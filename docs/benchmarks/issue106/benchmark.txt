Issue #106 - face-key masks for greedy meshing
==============================================

Change
------
Chunk::buildMeshRanged now materializes the face inputs of every slice cell
once into compact per-cell face keys (thread-local workspace, reserved per
worker; no per-slice/chunk heap churn), and the greedy width/height
expansion evaluates the merge rules on the key fields instead of
re-sampling both neighboring voxels and re-running the material/biome rules
per probe. The key carries the raw voxel PAIR plus both owner colors - not
just the cell's classified face - because the merge rules accept a
candidate whose own visible face is the opposite face of a different
transparent block, and they merge faces fronting air with faces fronting
transparent blocks; a pure "same face" equality key cannot express those
boundaries. AO/light stay out of the key (sampled at final quad corners).
An inert fast path (identical types or two different non-air opaque types -
no face possible from either side) skips color and classification work for
the majority of cells. Design documented in docs/engine-architecture.md
("Greedy meshing face keys").

Byte-identity proof
-------------------
tests/test_mesh_facekey --hash-corpus 25 97 (3 seeds x 25 strided chunks
with generated 4-neighborhood borders, full greedy + LOD hashes, plus one
600-edit noisy chunk per seed): output byte-identical before/after the
change (78/78 lines, every vertex/index count and FNV-1a payload hash
equal). Terrain, water/coast, forest foliage, biome tint, transparency and
chunk-border geometry are all covered by the corpus; the deterministic
scenes in the test pin each boundary class individually. The metadata-bounds
vs forced-full-range equivalence checks from #115 still pass on every scene.

Design iteration note
---------------------
The first key layout (one 64-bit "classified face" key compared by
equality) was +13% SLOWER on the corpus and, more importantly, changed
merge semantics: vertex counts shifted in both directions because the old
rules evaluate the candidate's voxel PAIR, not its classification. The
hash-corpus diff caught it immediately; the pair-carrying key above
restored byte-identity and the win.
